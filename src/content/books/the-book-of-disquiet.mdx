## Why I picked up this book

I wanted to read something that did not rush toward a conclusion.

A book made from fragments felt appropriate for a season of life that also felt unfinished and scattered.

## What stayed with me

Pessoa writes about solitude without making it feel empty.

The book notices small things:

- A room.
- A passing thought.
- A change in light.
- The strange distance between one person and another.

The writing feels like somebody thinking aloud in a quiet room.

> A book does not always need to give answers.

Sometimes it only needs to give us better questions.

## Notes while reading

I am reading this slowly, a few pages at a time.

The fragments are easy to underline, but harder to explain. They feel less like arguments and more like weather.

## Lessons

- Solitude can sharpen attention.
- Ordinary moments often contain the richest observations.
- A book does not need a traditional plot to change the way we think.
