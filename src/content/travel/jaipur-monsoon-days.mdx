## The city after rain

Jaipur changed after the first rain.

The streets became quieter. The old walls held the colour of the evening, and the air carried the smell of wet stone, tea, and earth.

I had come expecting to move through the city quickly. Instead, the rain created small pauses everywhere.

A shopkeeper stood beneath a wooden awning. Someone placed a cup of tea on a low wall. A scooter passed slowly through the reflected light.

The city seemed less interested in being visited than in being noticed.

## Amber light

The best moments were not landmarks.

They were the spaces between them: a quiet street, a conversation that lasted longer than expected, and the soft amber light settling over the old buildings.

Travel often gives us a different scale for ordinary life.

A familiar concern becomes smaller after a long walk in an unfamiliar place. A day becomes less about reaching the next destination and more about noticing where we are.

## What remained

I remember the rain most clearly.

Not as an interruption, but as a way the city revealed itself.

The streets became reflective. The colours deepened. People slowed down.

Some places stay with us because of what we planned to see. Others stay because of what happened when the plans were interrupted.
