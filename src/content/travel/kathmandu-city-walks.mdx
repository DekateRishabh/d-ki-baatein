## Before the city became busy

Kathmandu is easiest to remember in the morning.

The streets are still finding their rhythm. Shops are opening. Tea is being poured. The city is awake, but not yet hurried.

Walking without a fixed destination became the best way to understand it.

Every turn offered another detail: an old doorway, a courtyard, a small shrine, or a market beginning to gather around itself.

## Walking as attention

A city reveals itself through repetition.

The same street looks different when visited at another hour. The same square carries a different mood after rain. A familiar path becomes more detailed the longer we stay with it.

I found myself looking less for remarkable places and more for ordinary patterns.

A person arranging flowers.

A child crossing a courtyard.

The sound of metal shutters opening one by one.

## A city in fragments

No visit can contain an entire city.

What remains are fragments: early light, crowded lanes, quiet courtyards, and the feeling of being briefly included in a place that continues long after you leave.

Perhaps this is what travel gives us.

Not a complete understanding, but a few images that continue to change inside memory.
